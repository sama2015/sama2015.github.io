Music Masters

Web Design

Overview

Music Masters is a fictional online guitar shop created for a Web Design and Development course project. Our goal was to build a responsive site using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, offering a catalog of guitars and accessories. The site emphasizes user experience and product clarity.

Visit live site

Context, Skills, and Tools

ToolsFigma, HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript
TypeAcademic project (IAT 339)
DateJune–July 2025
TeamSam (myself), Muhammad Junaid
RoleUX Designer, Front-End Developer

Problem / Objective

We aimed to design a professional, easy-to-use website for selling guitars online. Many competitors had cluttered interfaces or limited mobile usability, so our challenge was to create a clean and responsive experience across devices.

Design and Iteration

Initial iteration

I led the wireframe creation in Figma, drafting the structure for the homepage, product page, and contact section. After team feedback, I refined spacing, visuals, and hierarchy for better clarity.

Style guide

I put together a style guide so fonts, colours, buttons, form fields, and imagery stayed consistent across every page.

Website iteration

For the final website, we decided to use solid colors for more visual consistency and easier readability for users.

Before

Gradient colors made the navigation buttons harder to see.

After

Made the color scheme solid to keep the layout consistent and improve visibility.

The products page had sections for different guitar types. After testing, it was hard to navigate between sections.

Added filter bars so users can jump to sections without scrolling.

Original product layout on the gradient colour scheme.

Final product cards on solid colours, with a clearer price and buttons.

Responsiveness

I designed the footer using Flexbox so the content wraps when the screen shrinks.

Hover effect

Added hover animations to the footer links so users understand they can be clicked.

Final Outcome

The final site was fully responsive, visually consistent, and supported smooth interaction across desktops, tablets, and phones. Our user testing confirmed ease of navigation and positive aesthetic feedback.

Takeaways

This project helped me develop a deeper understanding of how design and development choices affect user experience. Through wireframing and iteration, I learned the importance of planning before coding, especially how early structure can prevent issues later in the process. Designing in Figma let me quickly test ideas and share them with my team, leading to faster and more confident design decisions.

On the development side, building responsive layouts with Flexbox taught me how to make flexible components that adapt to different screen sizes. I also gained experience improving interactivity through hover states and subtle animations, which enhance usability without overwhelming the user.

Another key takeaway was the importance of consistency in branding and structure. Keeping fonts, colors, and layout patterns unified across the site made the interface feel more polished and professional. Small details like padding and alignment can significantly change how users perceive a website.

Acuva Brand Book

Graphic Design

Overview

Acuva Brand Book is a brand book project where we chose the water filtering company Acuva and rebranded it.

Context, Skills, and Tools

ToolsIllustrator, InDesign, Photoshop
TypeAcademic project (IAT 102)
DateMar–Apr 2023
TeamSam (myself)
RoleGraphic Designer

Problem / Objective

The objective was to make a logotype that represents the values of Acuva, a company that creates technology to purify water. Along with this, we created a brand book showing the logotype on products, letterheads and business cards, along with logotype variations and brochures.

Process

Logotype ideation

  • Created a mind map to get ideas on what to convey with the logotype and what the company does.
  • Drew different logotypes that could convey the company’s values.

Refined version

  • Sketched layouts for business cards and letterheads.

Logotype variations

  • Chose colour schemes based on different color wheels to match Acuva’s themes of water and technology.

Applying to the logotype

  • Applied these colors as individual layers to create each logotype, using InDesign.

Final Outcome

The result was a brand book containing relevant information about the company, along with the process behind the deliverables: the logotype, business cards, and letterhead.

Click a page to view it larger. Use the arrow keys or the buttons to move between pages.

Takeaways

This project gave me insight into the process of branding design, with an emphasis on ideation through mind maps, matrices, and drawing.

On the technical side, I learned how to create layouts and logos using InDesign and Illustrator, as well as kerning for my logotype.

Finally, it taught me the importance of user research. Researching the company’s goals and values informed my design decisions and made sure the design reflected the company’s identity.

Volunteer Fair Landing Page

The task was to create a clean, modern landing page mockup for one YRES program or event. It needed to be easy to understand and engaging for students, parents, and community members. I redesigned the Volunteer Fair page.
